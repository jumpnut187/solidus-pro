Sand Mold and Cores (MoldParams, core/mold_setup.py) — Behavior
=================================================================

The mold can be carried two ways:

Default: the explicit mold, generated automatically (auto_mold = 1), with
automatic cores (auto_cores = 1). Supplied geometry overrides both: a loaded
mold part replaces the automatic box, and loaded core parts replace the
automatic cores.

- Sand boundary (auto_mold 0 and no mold part). Metal surfaces lose
  heat through a Robin condition to the mold temperature with the
  semi-infinite sand penetration decay (ThermalBoundaryParams). Its 0.35
  air-gap floor (175 W/m²K after about 5 s) keeps the outer mold from ever
  saturating, so it runs fast: a 2 in WCB cube freezes in ~140 s.
- Explicit mold (default; a part typed Mold, or MoldParams.auto_mold). The mold is
  sand cells in the thermal solve, as MAGMASOFT meshes it: it heats up,
  saturates in corners and pockets, and its outer faces radiate and convect
  to the air. The same cube freezes in ~225 s, inside the 180-252 s
  Chvorinov estimate for hot PUNB silica (b² = 1.0-1.4e6).

1. Mold stage (core/simulation_pipeline.py::_prepare_mold_stage)
   Runs after part types are assigned, before filling and solidification,
   on the solver's own voxel lattice:
   - Mold box: the interior of loaded mold parts (flask, cope, drag;
     PartType.MOLD, code 10, never voxelised with the metal), or with
     auto_mold a box wall_thickness_in beyond the assembly. 0 (default)
     is automatic: 3 x the half-thickness of the heaviest metal section,
     0.75-8 in (auto_wall_thickness) -- three heat-penetration depths at the
     freeze time, since by Chvorinov sqrt(alpha t_f) is about the modulus
     for steel in silica. A 2 in WCB cube froze at 248 s with 2 in and with
     4 in walls; sand further out costs little on the graded mold (2b).
     MAGMASOFT's steel validation molds carry about 4 in.
   - Top of the box (mold_box): with open risers or pour cups, the cope is
     flush with the highest open top, raised where needed so blind risers
     and the casting keep closed_riser_cover_in (2 in) of sand over them.
     Without open tops: body tops plus the wall, blind risers plus the
     cover. An open body below the cope surface gets a shaft of air above
     it. Pour cups are always open; a riser is open when Part.is_open_top.
   - Gaps: a cell with assembly on both sides along an axis is a one-cell
     gap the lattice cannot resolve (bodies touching in CAD). It is neither
     sand nor air; the mesh's bridging links keep the contact.
   - Auto cores (auto_cores, skipped when any core part is loaded):
     core_rule_code 0 "draw" (default): sand with
     the assembly both above and below it in its column cannot be formed by
     a pattern drawn vertically out of cope and drag -- enclosed cavities,
     horizontal holes, undercuts. 1 "enclosed": MAGMASOFT 4.4's automatic
     core, a flood fill from the mold faces; only sand the outside cannot
     reach. Auto cores are appended to the voxel mesh as CORE voxels (full
     cells), so every core analysis applies; metal voxels lose the exterior
     area they showed to them.
   - The mold sand itself never enters the mesh: voxel_mesh.mold_layout
     carries it to the engine.

2. Thermal solve (core/mold_setup.py::build_thermal_extension,
   core/solidification.py::_build_coupled_mold, physics/mold_coupling.py)
   The mold is its own HeatTransferModel, coupled to the casting model
   (metal, chills, cores; mesh-sized, so every analysis and display is
   unchanged) by CoupledMoldSolver. Sand diffuses ~15x more slowly than
   steel, so the mold steps once a second of simulated time while the
   casting steps at its own limit; each coupled casting cell sees its mold
   cells as a sink held for the step, and the heat each pair carried,
   G (integral T_i dt - tau T_j), goes to the mold exactly (energy conserved
   to round-off). A 2 in WCB cube freezes within 1.6 % of the single-field
   solve; a small rigged box runs in ~15 s instead of ~52 s.
   - Mold to mold: the 27-point stencil. Core to mold: stencil pairs with the
     stencil's own link weights and htc_core_contact (1000 W/m²K,
     MAGMASOFT uses C500-C800).
   - Metal and chills to mold: contact pairs, conductance
     A / (dx_i/2k_i + 1/h + dx_j/2k_j). A is the cell's exterior area (the
     cut-cell CAD area, or one face per exposed face) shared over its
     exposed faces; a 2 in cube carries exactly 24 in².
       metal (casting, gating, filters) to sand: htc_cast_mold (1000) times
         the steel-sand air-gap curve below the solidus when
         interface_htc_curve = 1 (STEEL_SAND_AIR_GAP_CURVE, read from
         Williams/Hardin/Beckermann SFSA 2015 Fig. 3: 1000 above solidus,
         ~380 at 900 °C, ~50 at 600 °C), else constant ("C1000").
       risers: a riser in an insulating sleeve has no contact pair and no
         link to a core (riser_sleeve_adiabatic = 1, default since
         2026-11-11): the sleeve cuts it off from the sand, so the mold and
         the cores do not change how fast it freezes. It cools through the
         metal it feeds and through an open top. On a production rigging
         (six sleeved risers on riser posts) the leaking sleeve below froze
         every riser before the post under it; cut off, they freeze last.
         riser_sleeve_adiabatic = 0 brings the leaking sleeve back:
         h = riser_sleeve_htc (61.7)
         * M^-0.68, M the riser's modulus (in) over the area it shows the
         mold and air. A sleeve wall thickens with the riser; a constant
         coefficient gave MEF 1.31 on a 2 x 3 in riser but 1.15 at 6 x 9 in.
         Calibrated: MEF 1.309 / 1.313 / 1.304 at M = 0.375 / 0.75 / 1.125 in,
         the 1.3 core/surrogate/design_rules.py sizes risers for (measured
         insulating sleeves 1.07-1.28, Williams et al. 2016). The sand
         boundary's htc_riser 220 gives 1.39 there, but only 1.17 against
         explicit sand, where the sand resistance dominates.
         A riser part set to riser_sleeve "none" (bare sand riser) gets no
         sleeve: its contact is the casting's (htc_cast_mold with the
         air-gap curve). Without an explicit mold it takes htc_cast_interface
         and the casting's decay instead of htc_riser. "exothermic" is not an
         option: the exothermic model (ExothermicParams) only runs in
         schedule_legacy, not in thermal_coupled.
       riser posts: htc_riser_post (350).
       chills: htc_chill_mold (1500).
   - Air: faces with no cell beyond them (outer mold faces, shafts, open
     riser and pour cup tops) get BoundaryType.RADIATION: air_htc (10) plus
     eps*sigma*(T^4 - T_air^4), metal_emissivity 0.8, sand_emissivity 0.9,
     to ambient_temperature. MAGMASOFT's default outer boundary is the same
     radiation plus convection to ~20 °C air; it has no flask.
   - Sand cells start at ThermalBoundaryParams.mold_temperature.

2b. Graded mold (MoldParams.mold_grading_levels, mold_fine_band_cells;
   core/mold_setup.py::grade_mold)
   The mold sand is solved on cells of 1, 2, 4, ... up to 2^levels voxels
   per side (default 3: up to 8x), coarse away from the casting and voxel
   size where it touches the casting, risers, gating, chills, cores and
   gaps, as MAGMASOFT grades its mold mesh. 0 is the uniform lattice at the
   voxel size, bit for bit the mold before 2026-11-11.
   - Rule, on the solver lattice: dist = Chebyshev distance (cells) from each
     mold cell to the nearest mesh voxel or gap. A 2^l block aligned to the
     block grid becomes one cell when all its cells are mold sand with
     dist > t_l; t_1 = mold_fine_band_cells (default 4, at least 2), then
     GRADING_TRANSITION_CELLS (2) cells of each level before the next:
     t_l = t_(l-1) + 2 * 2^(l-1) (4, 8, 16 with the defaults). Blocks cut by
     the lattice edge, a shaft, a core or a loaded mold part's boundary stay
     finer. The block grid is anchored so the partition is mirror-symmetric
     about the lattice centre on axes with an even cell count.
   - Every metal, chill and core pair lands on a voxel-size cell (checked),
     so the casting side sees exactly the uniform mold's contacts.
   - Solver: one mold HeatTransferModel with per-cell sizes. Same-size
     neighbours use the 27-point stencil; cells of different size that share
     faces are joined by InterfaceLinks with infinite contact coefficient
     (MOLD_GRADING_LINK_HTC), i.e. two-point conduction
     A / (dx_i/2k_i + dx_j/2k_j) over the shared faces. Conservative; the
     stable step is still set by the voxel-size cells. Corner links across a
     size step are dropped (the link carries the whole normal flux), which
     lowers the tangential conductance in the one layer at each step.
   - Air faces are summed over each cell's lattice faces (same total area).
   - max_mold_cells still caps lattice cells: the dense layout arrays are
     lattice sized.
   - Accuracy (tests/verify_graded_mold.py, WCB, levels 3, band 4, against
     the uniform mold, 2026-11-11). Max |per-voxel end-time difference| as
     % of the freeze time, sand cells uniform -> graded:
       2 in cube, 2 in walls, 0.2 in     0.39 %    28 k ->  12 k (2.4x)
       2 in cube, 4 in walls, 0.2 in     0.39 %   131 k ->  20 k (6.4x)
       2 in cube, 2 in walls, 0.1 in     0.43 %   218 k ->  40 k (5.4x)
       2 in cube, 4 in walls, 0.1 in     0.43 %   1.02 M -> 71 k (14.3x)
       plate + sleeved riser, auto, 0.2  0.70 %   1.04 M -> 110 k (9.5x)
       L bracket, auto wall, 0.1 in      0.22 %   156 k ->  47 k (3.3x)
       thick-thin, auto wall, 0.1 in     0.69 %   1.08 M -> 85 k (12.6x)
     Mean differences 0.03-0.27 %. The graded mold runs slightly slow (the
     plate +0.7 %, 5437 -> 5475 s): coarse cells and the dropped corner links
     draw heat a little less well where heat reaches them. Band 2 broke 1 %
     (L bracket 1.75 %, plate 1.04-1.39 %); band 8 held 0.14 % at 5.5x on the
     plate. Levels above 3 changed nothing on these molds (walls up to 8 in).
     Mold solve time fell with the cell count (plate 27 s -> 10 s), but the
     casting model then dominates (whole run 50 s -> 32 s).

3. Sands (core/mold_materials.py)
   Codes are saved in projects; append only. 0 = custom constant sand
   (core_* params for cores; mold_thermal_conductivity / diffusivity for
   the mold). Database sands are TabulatedBody cells: k(T) and
   rho(T)*cp(T) every step, moved by enthalpy so a step across a latent
   peak still pays its heat.
     1 green sand (3.5 % water: 2257 kJ/kg over 100-104 °C, water cp below)
     2 silica, chemically bonded (Midea & Shah PUNB mold sand; default mold)
     3 silica core sand, fine (Midea & Shah furan core sand)
     4 ceramic (mullite beads)       5 chromite        6 zircon
     7 olivine                       8 shell sand      9 dry silica
    10 bauxite beads                11 alumina-silicate (andalusite)
    12 silicon carbide (aluminum chill sand, not for steel)
   Bonded silica tables are measured (AFS 2002). cp of the other sands is
   mineral calorimetry (NIST-JANAF, DSC) scaled to measured mold means;
   their k(T) curves are estimates anchored to single published values
   (about +-30 %), noted per sand. Chilling power sqrt(k rho cp) at 600 °C
   relative to bonded silica: chromite 1.25, zircon 1.26, olivine 1.13,
   ceramic 1.0. The often-quoted "zircon chills 4x quartz" is not borne
   out by measured data.

4. Display
   Mold parts draw purple and core parts green, translucent (0.28), while
   setting up, in both front ends; the automatic mold box and cores are
   previewed the same way (desktop: Simulation > Mold & Cores; web: Mold
   panel, POST /api/projects/<id>/mold-preview). All sand bodies are hidden
   while a result is on screen: the engineer reviews the casting, its riser
   posts and its risers.

5. Cost and limits
   - Run length: the Chvorinov horizon stretches 4x (EXPLICIT_MOLD_TIME_FACTOR,
     floor 2 x min_simulation_time); explicit-mold freezes run 1.6-2.5x
     longer than the boundary's (bronze L-bracket 285 s -> 694 s), and the
     old horizon cut runs off with liquid left.
   - Mold cells cost solver time like any cell: a 2 in cube with 4 in walls
     is 1.3 k metal and 131 k lattice sand cells at 0.2 in, 20 k graded
     (6.4x fewer; 14x at 0.1 in). The ratio falls with casting size.
     max_mold_cells (40 M lattice cells) stops a run before memory runs out. Sand cells
     update their properties only after moving 0.01 K and skip the enthalpy
     correction below 1e-3 K (Numba kernels), so sand that never warms costs
     only the conduction stencil. A small rigged training box (22 k metal,
     619 k sand cells at 0.055 in) runs in ~27 s against ~5 s on the sand
     boundary.
   - Surrogate records carry explicit_mold; the training set in
     data/surrogate_dataset was regenerated on the explicit mold on
     2026-10-24 (the boundary-mold set is kept in
     data/surrogate_dataset_boundary_mold_2026-10-24).
   - The mold is graded (2b); mold_grading_levels = 0 is the uniform
     lattice. Convergence of the explicit cube on the uniform mold, as first
     recorded: 248 s (0.2 in), 228 s (0.1 in), 224 s (0.067 in); measured
     2026-11-11: 240.9 s (0.2 in), 220.6 s (0.1 in), graded 240.9 / 221.1 s.
   - Moisture migration in green sand (the condensation zone) and binder
     burn-off gas are not modelled thermally; only the evaporation heat.
   - Auto cores of the draw rule assume a vertical draw (z up) and an
     optimal parting surface.
   - tests/test_mold_setup.py covers all of the above.
